A neutral ruler for cell data.

An open reference for what “normal” cells look like in every lab, held by a nonprofit.

← all documents · Kat Titterton · San Francisco

01The moment

Biology is pooling cell data to train AI.
Nobody calibrates it across the labs that make it.

$1.8B
Biohub’s virtual-cell initiative with DeepMind, Meta, DOE, NIH [1]
120M
open single-cell data points from Tahoe, Arc and Biohub [2]
$30–50M
a typical FRO: 5 years, 15–30 people [3]

Building models: Biohub, Arc, Xaira, Noetik, Rivercell. Calibrating across labs: nobody yet.
The gap: a model names the lab from control wells alone, 99.9% vs 9.1% chance (public JUMP data).

02Why an FRO

Neutral

The holder of a reference must not profit from the answer.

Unbuilt

Consortia publish but don’t refresh; platforms erase the lab signal.

Proven

EvE Bio, a Convergent FRO, already runs a public drug–target dataset [4].

Fundable

ARIA and Convergent launched two UK FROs in April 2026; NSF is piloting up to $1B for labs like this [6][7].

03Two layers

NONPROFIT FRO

  • map of every lab’s controls
  • the standard for drift
  • open benchmark
→map
←licence
MEMBER SPIN-OUT
Membership
  • labs that share join free
  • screening teams pay per line
  • AI teams pay by API

Open: map, standard, code.
Paid: private-batch checks, API.
Only control wells are shared.
Each lab keeps its data and a share.
