Control is an illusion.

Confidence without control: scientific background and industry position.

← all documents · Kat Titterton · San Francisco · 25 September 2026

Abstract

Every result in cell biology is a difference from a control, and nothing checks the control against anyone else’s. On the public JUMP Cell Painting dataset, a classifier names the lab from untreated wells alone 99.9% of the time against 9.1% chance, so “normal” is a lab-specific coordinate, not a shared one. The normalisations that make cross-lab discovery possible erase that coordinate (centring each plate on its own controls drops lab identity to 24%): right for discovery, wrong for quality control. Datum Line keeps the coordinate and uses it. A lab’s control-well images are placed against the published field and every lab running the same line, and the lab gets back a certificate, a distance, and the direction of any shift. The bet still to be proven is that label-free brightfield carries the same signal as five-stain fluorescence; the test is specified and pending.

1The control problem

A control removes what a plate shares, including the plate’s own state, so a sick plate hides behind its own controls. Write a measured well as a shared mean, a plate state, and a treatment effect that may depend on that state:

xtest = μ + s + τ(s)xctrl = μ + sΔ = xtest − xctrl = τ(s)(1)

The state s cancels from the number but not from the cells. If s is off, Δ is still computed, still looks clean, and is measured on cells that are not the ones the field has in mind.

The same logic nests upward: well inside plate, plate inside batch, batch inside site, site inside the field. Each control sees only inside its own box. A whole plate off is visible to the batch; a whole site off is visible to nobody in the building. Only a reference outside every box, the field, can see where a lab sits.

This is not an exotic failure. Irreproducible preclinical research costs about $28B a year in the US alone, and the largest single contributor, about 36%, is biological reagents and reference materials [1]. Cells are the input nobody certifies against anyone else’s.

2Morphological profiling as a sensor

An image of a cell is an unbiased, many-dimensional sensor: it answers “is anything about this run off?” rather than one hand-picked question. Cell Painting multiplexes six fluorescent dyes in five channels to mark eight cellular components, and automated analysis measures about 1,500 features per cell: size, shape, texture, intensity [2].

At scale, the reference already exists. The JUMP Cell Painting Consortium (ten pharmaceutical companies, six technology partners, two non-profits) profiled over 116,750 compounds plus gene knockouts and overexpressions, all in U2OS cells [3]. Its main compound set, cpg0016, was produced across 11 sites, which is exactly the variation a cross-lab reference needs, and it is public under CC0 [3].

The sensor is not the bottleneck. Many widely used bioactivity benchmarks are predicted well by simply counting cells, because their assays track cell health [5]. When a naive baseline matches a model, the comparison is the problem, not the model. In the author’s hepatocyte work, three feature extractors (CellProfiler, a CNN, a ViT) performed similarly [6]. The asset is the reference, not the extractor.

Space. The batch above passes the check its own lab runs, yet sits further from the lab’s own June baseline than from another lab’s centre. Local QC cannot see this by construction (section 1).

Time. Within one lab, the distance between two plates grows from 0.63 in the same batch to 3.09 at 6 to 13 weeks and 4.7 beyond. It had not levelled off in four months, so a lab’s own history stops being a valid comparison within weeks.

Why correction is not the answer. Batch correction for image-based profiling has been benchmarked on JUMP across ten methods [4]; its purpose is to remove lab and batch differences so biology can be compared. That is right for discovery. For quality control it deletes the one coordinate that says where a lab sits.

4Formalism

Everything in section 3 is one of six operations on the same well vector, each a difference of means or a held-out classifier, so any lab can check it. Every well becomes an embedding, z-scored across the field:

e = φ(image) ∈ ℝ3773φ ∈ {CellProfiler, CNN, ViT}(2)
OperationDefinitionRole
1 · Embeddinge = φ(image)the sensor; choice of φ barely matters [6]
2 · Lab classifierf : e → labdoes the lab leave a fingerprint (99.9%)
3 · Certificate distanceeq. (3)how far a plate sits from the field, and which way
4 · Variance decompositionVar(e) = Vmodel + Vsite + Vbatch + Vplate + Vwellhow much is instrument, site, batch
5 · Residualize known variablese⊥ = e − Ê[e | c]remove only what is known to be instrument
6 · Morphology changeδ = f(‖e − E[e | vehicle]‖)size of a shift, for the known-answer test

The certificate is a difference of two means in units of the field’s spread, conditioned on the recorded variables c (cell line, plate type, microscope, and so on):

d = ‖ E[e | plate] − E[e | field, c] ‖Σ(3)

Its direction names the features that moved; its negative points back toward the field. “Normal” is therefore a conditional expectation, E[x | every variable], and it is only as good as the variables recorded. JUMP records the instrument side (site, batch, plate type, microscope, objective, filter set, pixel size); it does not publicly record serum or media lot, passage, seeding density, dye lots or operator.

Discovery and QC want opposite things from the same vector. Discovery wants invariance to the lab, so it aligns every plate to a reference before comparing. QC wants the lab coordinate, so it must never align it away.

6Industry position

The market checks cell identity and ignores cell behaviour. Datum Line is the behaviour half, held by nobody who sells cells, media or instruments.

Who pays. Academic labs and cores, on every new line (at stake: the paper). Pharma and CRO screening groups, on every transfer (at stake: the screen; one line checked late cost about $2.6M in the author’s experience: 3 runs a week for 17 weeks at about $50k a run). Cell banks and vendors, on every lot (at stake: the lot and every lab that bought it).

Why it compounds. Day one needs no network: the public JUMP reference already holds eleven labs’ U2OS controls, so the first customer is compared with the field, not with an empty database. Every upload widens the reference, and bad-lot detection needs many labs on the same lot, so the later products exist only once the network does.

Price. Per line, per quarter, as a starting cadence; the drift measured so far argues for more often. The figure is not yet set. What leaves the building: untreated control-well images only; no compounds, targets or results.
