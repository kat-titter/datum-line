Vision

Every lab that images cells should know where its cells sit among everyone else’s, and every dataset made from cells should carry that answer with it. Datum Line is one shared repository of raw cell images from every lab, on one map, and the product is a membership to it. Labs join free by sharing the control images they already take, and keep a share of what their data earns. Teams that use the data pay: screening teams first, for a check on every batch they run; AI teams the most, for aligned images from every lab by API. The arrow after that is left open on purpose.

The habit comes first, the way Strava’s segments place a run among everyone who ran the same stretch of road. Here the road is the cell line, and the field is every lab that ran it. Everything below serves one rule: uploading must be effortless, and the answer must be worth looking at.

01Why now

Cell data has a price. One vendor pays its experts more than $1.5M a day, about $85 an hour on average, at a $10B valuation. [10] Roche and Genentech paid Recursion a second $30M milestone for an accepted whole-genome map of microglial immune cells; $213M from the partnership to date. [11] A $1.8B virtual-cell data initiative, part new money and part existing funding; Meta, Google DeepMind and Isomorphic put in $300M together and get a year of exclusive access first. [12]

Cell data does not convert. EU-OPENSCREEN ran one Cell Painting protocol at 4 sites, with cells, serum and dye lots supplied centrally. Within a site, 84 to 94% of compounds replicate; across sites, 54 to 68% pair, and the authors call the profiles “not directly comparable between imaging sites” [13]. On the public JUMP data, a classifier names the lab from DMSO wells alone at 99.9% against 9.1% chance. Whoever makes cell data comparable sets its exchange rate.

04Six magic moments

Each moment turns an image the lab already has into something it could not see before, and each is ordered by when a new member meets it.

# Moment What the lab sees When
1 Your past, diagnosed old plates dragged in on day one appear as a path across the field map: "you left the envelope in March" first ten minutes
2 Show the cells, not the score when a plate moves, its nuclei next to their nearest neighbours from labs in distribution: same size, more texture every flag
3 The fix what the in-distribution labs do differently: seeding density, media or serum lot after a flag
4 A forecast "at your current drift, this line leaves the envelope in about five weeks" after three readings
5 Someone else's plate saves your screen a lot you use is drifting at other labs, before your own plates show it once labs share lots
6 The check as currency a verifiable link for a methods section, a CRO handoff, a grant report when results leave the lab

Moments 1 to 4 work for a single lab on day one, against the public reference. Moments 5 and 6 exist only once the network does, and no competitor can copy them without the labs.

05Membership

Membership is access to the repository for a period, not ownership of the data. A lab that shares joins free; everyone else pays by how they use it. The check on every batch is what screening teams pay for first; aligned images from every lab are what AI teams pay the most for.

What never gets sold. A lab’s treated data, its results, its name without consent, a ranking of instruments, or a verdict for hire. Membership buys access and the check; it never buys a better verdict. Prices and shares are proposals (boards A15 and A22) until the first partner labs and the first members set them.

07The game

Every batch a lab sends is ranked against every batch the field has seen, on its DMSO wells alone: how typical the cells are, how alike the plates are, how steady the lab is from batch to batch, and cells per well against the field. Nothing in the score touches a treated well or a result, so the only way to climb is better culture and imaging practice. On the public data, lab 5 holds the field’s longest streak, 33 batches (results/e14). A lab sees its rank, its personal best, its streak and the lab to beat.
