Identity, yes. Behaviour, no.

Who checks cells today, what nobody sells, and who we make stronger.

← all documents · Kat Titterton · San Francisco · 25 September 2026

Everyone adjacent certifies something about cells. Nobody certifies that cells behave like the field's version of the same line. Identity services prove what a line is. Instruments and AI platforms measure cells against the lab's own controls. Batch correction removes the lab-to-lab signal on purpose. Datum's product is the one thing none of them can sell: a neutral, cross-lab reference for what normal looks like, refreshed on a measured schedule.

Identity and contamination testing

This is the closest existing habit, and it answers a different question. ATCC's human STR service fingerprints a line's DNA and compares it with a reference profile; it also sells PCR mycoplasma testing [1][2]. ATCC services · STR method

  • What it proves: the cells are the line on the label, and they are clean.
  • What it can't see: two correctly identified, mycoplasma-free cultures of the same line that behave differently. ATCC's own guidance cites low- and high-passage LNCaP cells responding divergently to the same hormones, which is exactly the gap. ATCC
  • Habit worth borrowing: ATCC tells labs to re-test on receipt, after ten passages, after banking and "when in doubt". Labs already accept a recurring check. ATCC blog
  • Relationship to Datum: complement, not competitor. Identity first, behaviour second. Pitch Datum as the second line on the same certificate a lab already files.
  • Neutrality: ATCC distributes the cells it authenticates, which is fine for identity and a conflict for behaviour.

Live-cell and label-free imaging instruments

Instrument makers already sell label-free AI. Sartorius, the clearest example checked here, references the lab's own wells. Sartorius's Incucyte AI Cell Health module classifies live and dead cells from unlabelled images with a pretrained network. Its Advanced Label-Free Classification add-on trains a classifier on the plate's own live and dead control wells. The 2024B release added normalising to specific wells [5][6][7]. AI Cell Health · Label-free classification · v2024B

  • What they prove: what happened on this plate, relative to this plate.
  • What they can't see: slide 03's bug. If the control wells drifted, the classifier learned the drift.
  • Threat: highest capability to copy the analytics. Lowest credibility as the holder, because the answer "your cells drifted" can land on their own instrument.
  • Relationship to Datum: channel partner. Brightfield and phase images from installed instruments are the cheapest possible input to a certificate. The long game is to be the reference every instrument vendor reads from.

Phenomics and AI drug discovery platforms

These companies have the most images and the best models, and they treat lab-to-lab variation as noise to remove. Recursion trains the Phenom models on its own microscopy and released OpenPhenom-S/16, trained on RxRx3 and JUMP. The model card tells users to batch-correct embeddings with TVN, fit on control images across batches, before the features work [8][9]. Recursion release · OpenPhenom model card

Ardigen's phenAID compares client Cell Painting data with annotated reference profiles to predict activity and mechanism, and hosts a JUMP data explorer [10][11]. phenAID · JUMP explorer

  • What they prove: what a compound does, after the lab signal is scrubbed out.
  • What they can't see: the scrubbed signal. Appendix A3 shows that centring a plate on its own controls drops lab identity from 99.9% to 24%; that is the coordinate a certificate needs.
  • Threat: Recursion could publish a reference. It would be tied to its own lines, conditions and incentives.
  • Relationship to Datum: customers and data partners. Their models get better inputs when the plates feeding them are certified.
  • Adjacent, not listed as competitors: Axiom Bio builds liver-toxicity prediction on primary hepatocyte imaging. biopharmatrend It is a drug-safety product, not a cell-state reference.

Open consortia, public data and batch-correction methods

The open world built the evidence and then, by design, threw away the product. JUMP brought ten pharma companies, six technology companies and two non-profits together to image U2OS cells under one protocol [13]. JUMP A published benchmark of batch correction on that data tested ten methods and found Harmony and Seurat RPCA best at removing batch effects [14]. Arevalo 2024 In ML, WILDS includes cross-experiment cell imaging (RxRx1) among its distribution-shift benchmarks [15]. WILDS

  • What they prove: the effect is real and large.
  • What they don't do: run a service, refresh a reference, or stand behind an answer for a specific lab.
  • Relationship to Datum: foundation and first allies. Build on JUMP, cite it, and offer the consortium the certificate as its natural next step.

Standards bodies and reference materials

The standards world already made identity a norm, and it did so with a neutral partner. ATCC developed its mouse STR profiling with NIST and tested it across ten independent labs [4]. ATCC standards The US cost of irreproducible preclinical work was estimated at about US$28B a year, with reagents and reference materials, including misidentified lines, the largest category [17]. PLoS Biology · Science News

  • Relationship to Datum: the path to a behavioural standard runs through the same kind of body. Plan for a standards partner, not a standards fight.
  • Open question: whether a phenotypic reference material, a physical control line run by every lab, belongs in the product.

Why neutrality is the moat, and who moves next

The model is not the moat; the reference is, and only a neutral party can hold it. The loop only turns if nobody fears the holder. That rules out anyone who sells cells, media, instruments or drug programs.

Short-term loss that buys a structural win: give the reference's summary statistics away to the open community, and sell the per-lab, per-quarter certificate.
