Twice the size of its neighbours

Treat a plate of U2OS cells with AMG 900 and one nucleus in five comes out more than twice the area of a vehicle-treated one. The drug never touches DNA. It binds a kinase with about an hour of work to do, once per cell cycle, and only during mitosis. Take that hour away and the nuclei swell. The route from one to the other is short, and most of it has been measured.

01

Amgen built it to get past the pumps

AMG 900 is a phthalazine out of Amgen’s Aurora programme. Its more soluble predecessor had lost potency against multidrug-resistant lines. So the chemists went back down the polarity scale and took the metabolic hit that came with it; the compound they settled on, numbered 23r in that paper, is AMG 900.1 Six rings, 503.6 Da.2

It inhibited 26 tumour cell lines at low nanomolar concentrations, including lines resistant to paclitaxel and to three other Aurora inhibitors, and it worked in a variant carrying an Aurora‑B mutation that defeats AZD1152.3 It reached phase 1. In solid tumours the maximum tolerated dose was 25 mg/day, 40 with G‑CSF support, and 3 of 29 evaluable patients with ovarian cancer had a partial response.4 In AML, 3 of 35 patients reached a complete response with incomplete count recovery.5 No regulator approved it and development stopped there. Its steadiest job now is as a positive control in image-based screens, one of the compounds a plate is expected to react to.6 That is a quieter career than Amgen had in mind, and it is also why the mechanism is so well described: plenty of people have since looked hard at what this molecule does to a cell.

02

Three kinases, and one of them explains the nuclei

Aurora A, B and C are serine/threonine kinases that run cell division. Reported inhibition by AMG 900 is 5, 4 and 1 nM.7 Aurora B is the one doing the work in this story.

It does not work alone. Aurora B travels with INCENP, survivin and borealin as the chromosomal passenger complex, which parks at the inner centromere and stays there through metaphase.8 From that seat it holds three jobs at once. It pulls apart kinetochore–microtubule attachments that are wrong. It keeps the spindle assembly checkpoint signalling for as long as any attachment is still wrong. And it builds the contractile ring that cuts the cell in two. Error correction, the alarm and the purse-string, all on one kinase.

AMG 900 occupies its ATP pocket. What you can measure afterwards is a phosphate that is no longer there. Serine 10 of histone H3 is Aurora B’s own substrate, and oral dosing in mice cut p‑histone H3 in tumour xenografts by more than 99%.9

03

Checkpoint off, furrow never built

Two inhibitors from 2003 settled what a cell does when Aurora B stops. Cells given ZM447439 went through interphase, entered mitosis and built a bipolar spindle, all on schedule. Then alignment failed, segregation failed, and cytokinesis failed. The cells left mitosis on time anyway, because BubR1, Mad2 and CENP‑E no longer loaded onto the kinetochores that should have been raising the alarm.10 Hesperadin gave the same result from the other direction: anaphase with chromosomes still hanging off one pole, and cells arrested by taxol released inside an hour.11

AMG 900 produces the same picture. Payton’s group named it plainly: aborted cell division without a prolonged mitotic arrest.3 By then the DNA has already been copied. The spindle does not separate it properly, no furrow forms, and one nuclear envelope reassembles around all of it.

04

4N, then 8N

What re-enters G1 is one cell carrying 4N DNA in one nucleus. The checkpoint that would stop it going round again is the one Aurora B was running, so it goes round again: 8N, then 16N. In AML lines this was counted by flow cytometry as the fraction of cells with ≥4N DNA content, rising in step with the loss of p‑histone H3 and alongside p53 and cleaved caspase‑3. Where p21 came up instead, cells stalled at a clean 4N peak and went no further.12

Nuclear size follows DNA content, and that link has been tested on its own rather than assumed. In Xenopus egg extracts, with the cytoplasm held constant and the DNA content varied, both the size the nucleus settled at and the speed at which it expanded tracked the amount of DNA inside it.13 How far a frog extract carries into a human osteosarcoma cell is a fair question. It is not a fixed ratio either. In cancer cells that have doubled their genome, volume does not always scale with content, and the ones that stay small turn out to be the fitter ones.14 The direction holds. More DNA inside one envelope, a larger envelope.

05

What the plate shows

Three wells of the JUMP Cell Painting pilot plate make the whole argument visible: two vehicle wells and one treated with AMG 900 at 10 µM, U2OS cells, Hoechst for DNA.15,16 Segmenting 1,092 nuclei across three fields per well gives a median nuclear area of 176 µm² under vehicle and 258 µm² under AMG 900, and integrated Hoechst signal per nucleus 1.63× higher. One in five treated nuclei is more than twice the vehicle median area. Under vehicle, fewer than one in a hundred is.

So the chain is measured end to end, almost. The drug in the pocket, the lost phosphate in tissue, the ≥4N DNA by flow cytometry, the larger nuclei here. One link is joined rather than shown, and it is the limit of this plate: nobody counted chromosomes in these wells. What makes it the same story is that the DNA signal rose along with the area, in the same nuclei. A ploidy stain, or live imaging of a division that fails to finish, would settle it properly.

AMG 900 never touched the DNA. It took away the hour in which a cell checks its own work before splitting in two, so the cell stopped splitting, and the DNA stayed where it was — all of it, inside one envelope. That is the one nucleus in five you are looking at.
