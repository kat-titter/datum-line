<h1>Gates before <span class="acc">money.</span></h1><p class="sub">Phases, the raise, early hires and <span class="grn">how it earns</span>.</p><div class="byline"><a class="back" href="./">&#8592; all documents</a> &#183; Kat Titterton &#183; San Francisco &#183; 25 September 2026</div></header><h2>Summary</h2>
<p>The cheapest tests are also the ones that could kill the company, so they run first, on public data, before any money is raised. Once they pass, raise about <strong>$3M on a post-money SAFE</strong> to fund 24 months: four early hires, a three-site proof, and the reference built to break. The seed round after that is raised on proof that labs upload and certificates agree, not on the science alone.</p>
<p>All dollar figures here are proposals derived from public benchmarks and salary norms, not quotes; market medians are cited where used.</p>

<h2><span class="num">02</span>The raise</h2>
<p><strong>Recommendation: about $3M on a post-money SAFE, raised right after phase 0 passes, to fund 24 months through phase 2.</strong> Run the kill tests first (weeks, not months, and nearly free), then raise on evidence instead of a hypothesis.</p>
<p>Why that size: the $1–2.5M middle of the pre-seed market is thinning, down from 24% of rounds in Q1 2023 to 18% in Q1 2026 [1], and once a financing reaches about $3M, priced rounds become as common as SAFEs [2]. So $3M sits just above the squeezed middle while keeping the simpler instrument. For scale, aggregators of Carta data put the mid-2026 median US priced seed at $4.1M on a $24.3M post-money, about 18% dilution [4].</p>

<p><strong>Where to look.</strong> ML: alumni of cell-profiling groups and of phenomics or ML-for-biology companies, and academic groups working on distribution shift. Assay: pharma screening groups, CROs, and core facility staff. Partnerships: field application scientists at imaging and cell companies.</p>
<p><strong>Advisors, before hire 4.</strong> A bioimage-informatics academic from the JUMP circle (credibility and the reference); a head of screening at a pharma company (the buyer's view); a quality and standards expert who has run identity testing to published standards [5]; someone from a cell bank or vendor (the channel). Plus the employment counsel you already use, to keep the company clear of any prior-employer IP question.</p>
<p><strong>Not yet.</strong> A CEO hire, a sales team, or a wet lab of your own: cores and CROs cover bench work until the planted-failure set proves what an in-house lab would add.</p>
<h2><span class="num">05</span>Revenue model</h2>
<p>Identity testing already has a price: $217 per human STR sample at ATCC [5], $255 with a database search at the Johns Hopkins core [6]. That buys one identity check. The certificate is a behaviour check repeated every quarter, so it sells as a subscription per cell line per quarter; the quarter comes from the measured drift plateau.</p>

<p><strong>Unit economics (derived, not measured).</strong> One pharma site with 10 active lines at $5k per line-quarter is \~$200k a year; twenty such sites is \~$4M a year before vendor or alert revenue. Against a single \~$2.6M failure, $50k a year is under 2% of the loss it prevents. For the size of the whole problem, about $28B a year of US preclinical research is irreproducible, with reagents and reference materials the largest share [7].</p>
<p><strong>What never gets sold.</strong> Rankings of instruments, or the right for a vendor to grade its own lot. The benchmark can be licensed; the verdict cannot.</p>
